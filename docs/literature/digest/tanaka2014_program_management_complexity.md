# Tanaka (2014) — Toward project and program management paradigm in the space of complexity: a case study of mega and complex oil and gas development and infrastructure projects

**Full citation:** Tanaka, H. (2014). *Toward project and program management paradigm in the space of complexity: a case study of mega and complex oil and gas development and infrastructure projects*. Procedia - Social and Behavioral Sciences, 119, pp. 65–74. 27th IPMA World Congress. DOI: `10.1016/j.sbspro.2014.03.010`. Open access, CC BY-NC-ND. Author: Hiroshi Tanaka, Japan Advanced Institute of Science and Technology; self-described 42 years of experience in the engineering-procurement-construction (EPC) industry for oil, gas, and infrastructure projects.

## Problem
Project-management research has not kept pace with the rapid scaling and rising complexity of mega oil-and-gas and infrastructure development projects. The paper asks how project/program management theory should evolve to handle projects characterized by multiple, sometimes misaligned stakeholder objectives, evolving technology, uncertain environments, and scalability — i.e., genuine "complexity," not just size.

## Method
Qualitative, practitioner-based research: (1) continues monitoring "PESTLE" (political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental) complexity events from the author's earlier (2012/2013) paper to check whether the original impact analysis still holds; (2) reviews a set of ongoing mega oil-and-gas and infrastructure projects reported in the press/industry sources for their multi-objective, multi-stakeholder, complexity characteristics; (3) reviews project-management literature on complex projects (including citing Linkov (2006) — see this folder's `linkov2006_mcda_adaptive_management.md` — specifically for its treatment of comparative risk assessment moving to MCDA and adaptive management, applied here to complex-project risk); (4) derives new conceptual "thoughts" toward a program-management paradigm.

## Key results / numbers
No quantitative model; the contribution is conceptual. Four "new thoughts" are proposed (Section 4, PDF p. 6–8): (1) **meta program management** — a "centrally sponsored group of programs and projects... delivered by discrete self-governing organizations, centrally coordinated to achieve strategically important outcomes" (quoting Morey 2011), needed because traditional project/program governance, built on positivist delivery-focused techniques, cannot handle multiple, not-always-aligned stakeholder objectives; (2) knowledge- and stakeholder-integration platforms (a "mission-profiling platform" combining existing/new knowledge, stakeholder capabilities/funds, financing options, and delivery-model choices such as FEED→EPC or PPP); (3) finance planning/structuring as an essential ingredient of materializing large projects, given very large capital requirements (illustrated with Asian Development Bank infrastructure-investment figures); (4) contingent/adaptive risk management for "wicked" (unstructured) project risks, citing Kämpf & Haley's (2011) work on Arctic offshore risk management as a strong example of this approach.

## Relevance to our case
Used only as a *framing analogy*: it supports treating the orbital fuel-depot buildout (Earth-New option exercise, Lunar-ISRU pilot commissioning, phased CAPEX) in the management note as a complex, multi-stakeholder capital program rather than a single deterministic optimization problem, and supports linking the risk register / stakeholder analysis to an adaptive/contingent risk-management lens (paralleling this case's mandatory stress scenario and risk register requirements).

## Quotable statements
- "[P]roject management research has not caught up with this rapid development of the project industry... due to a time lag usually occurring between the state of the projects and scientific research." (PDF p. 2 / journal p. 66)
- Meta program management defined (quoting Morey 2011) as "a centrally sponsored group of programs and projects that are delivered by a number of discrete self-governing organizations, and centrally coordinated to achieve outcomes that are of strategic importance." (PDF p. 7 / journal p. 71)
- Cites "transition from comparative risk assessment to multi-criteria decision analysis and adaptive management in complex environmental policy design (Linkov, 2006)" as one of the strongest inputs to a new contingent risk-management paradigm for complex projects. (PDF p. 9 / journal p. 73)

## Cautions
- Entirely qualitative and terrestrial (oil & gas / large infrastructure); no cost, schedule, or capacity figures transferable to this case.
- The "review of ongoing mega projects" is a media/press-based qualitative survey by one practitioner-author, not a peer-reviewed empirical study — treat its factual claims about specific named projects as illustrative context, not verified data.
- Filename in the organizer's folder ("...space_oil...") is a topical label chosen by whoever compiled the folder, not part of the paper's own title; the actual title concerns oil & gas / infrastructure megaprojects, used here purely as a management-paradigm analogy for a space fuel-depot program.
