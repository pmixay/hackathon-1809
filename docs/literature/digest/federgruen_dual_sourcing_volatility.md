## Problem
Studies a single-item, periodic-review dual-sourcing inventory problem in "an increasingly volatile world," distinguishing two kinds of volatility: *external* volatility (a Markov-modulated macro "state-of-the-world" driving fluctuations in both suppliers' cost structures, capacity limits, supply mechanisms, and demand — e.g., tariffs, labor-cost indices, currency/interest rates) and *internal* volatility (randomness in what is actually delivered given an order — random yields, disruptions, quality failures, capacity shortfalls).

## Method
A Markov-decision-process-style periodic-review model: each of the two supply sources has its own cost parameters, lead time, per-period capacity limit, and random supply (yield/curtailment) mechanism, all of which may depend on the evolving state-of-the-world (a finite-state Markov chain). Unmet demand backlogs; leftover inventory can be carried at a holding cost or salvaged. Under the assumption that the two suppliers' lead times are "consecutive" (differ by exactly one period), the authors derive the structure of the jointly optimal ordering-plus-salvaging policy and show it collapses to a tractable, low-dimensional (modified base-stock-type) form, in contrast to the general case (lead-time difference > 1), which requires a full vector-valued state and loses this tractability (PDF p. 31, "Conclusions and Generalizations").

## Key results / numbers
No case-specific numeric constants; the paper's own numerical studies (referenced but not fully extracted here) are used to illustrate and stress-test the structural/comparative claims, not to produce transferable figures. The paper's headline qualitative/managerial finding: exploiting dual sourcing, a firm can actually *benefit* from increased external volatility rather than simply absorbing it as extra cost — "benefits increase as volatilities increase in specific ways" (abstract) — a result the authors describe as counter to some "reasonable conjectures" that their numerical studies reject. Internal volatility is treated as behaving more conventionally (a pure source of added cost/risk) in their comparison of the two volatility types.

## Relevance to our case
Supports keeping two conceptually distinct sources of uncertainty separate in TerraPlan's risk register and sensitivity analysis: (a) *channel-internal* risk — e.g., Lunar-ISRU ramp-up reliability (0.78/0.90/0.93 across 2038-2040) or any single channel's delivery shortfall — versus (b) *system-external* volatility — e.g., overall demand growth uncertainty or economy-wide price/tariff shifts affecting multiple channels at once. Also lends conceptual (not numeric) support to treating the 5-channel portfolio's value as partly coming from the *flexibility* to shift volume between channels as conditions change, not only from each channel's nominal unit price.

- "[T]he firm can benefit from external volatilities; indeed, benefits increase as volatilities increase in specific ways. Numerical studies illustrate these results and reject other reasonable conjectures." (abstract, PDF p. 1)
